Judge the step's prose — not the heading, not a fenced code block or ASCII
diagram, not the closing question and its escape-hatch line.

The prose is tight and easy to follow. It teaches one idea and stops; a
reader gets it on one pass without wading. The skill's own budget is about
150 words, and that is the target, but this check is about readability
rather than a word count, so a step that runs somewhat over while staying
clear and unpadded passes.

Do not attempt an exact count. Judge whether the step reads tight.

Fail if the prose sprawls — well past the budget, two ideas stacked into
one step, restatement or padding, or a reader having to work to find the
point.
